# CHAPTER FIVE: CONCLUSION AND RECOMMENDATIONS

## 5.1 Introduction

This is the final chapter of my project report. In this chapter, I summarize what I accomplished throughout this study. I also present my conclusions about the project and give recommendations for future improvements.

The purpose of this project was to design and implement a web-based car parts availability and price comparison system for spare parts vendors in Yaoundé, Cameroon. I named the system SparePlug. Through this chapter, I reflect on what I learned and how the system can be improved in the future.

## 5.2 Summary

Throughout this project, I followed a systematic approach to build the SparePlug system. Let me summarize what I did in each chapter.

In **Chapter One**, I introduced the study by explaining the background of the spare parts market in Cameroon. I identified the problem at John Automobile, where manual systems caused slow service and price inconsistency. I set my main objective to design and implement a web-based system that helps vendors manage their inventory and helps customers find parts easily.

In **Chapter Two**, I reviewed relevant literature about e-commerce, price comparison systems, and technology acceptance. I also presented my internship experience at N-Tec Labs, where I gained practical skills that helped me in this project. The theoretical frameworks guided my design decisions.

In **Chapter Three**, I explained the methodology I used to build the system. I described the data collection methods, functional requirements, and technical specifications. I applied the UML method to create use case diagrams and activity diagrams. I also documented the database structure and the software tools I used.

In **Chapter Four**, I presented the results of the implementation. I showed the main screens of the system including the home page, search results, product details, vendor dashboard, and admin panel. I documented the testing results, which showed high success rates across different test types. I also presented scenarios that demonstrated how the system works in real situations.

## 5.3 Conclusion

Based on my work throughout this project, I draw the following conclusions:

**Conclusion 1:** The SparePlug system successfully addresses the problem of price inconsistency in the Yaoundé car parts market. By displaying prices from different vendors side by side, buyers can now compare and choose the best deal. This was one of the main problems I identified at John Automobile.

**Conclusion 2:** The vehicle compatibility feature is a valuable addition that sets SparePlug apart from other solutions. By allowing users to filter parts by their specific car make, model, and year, the system ensures that buyers find parts that actually fit their vehicles. This reduces the frustration of buying wrong parts.

**Conclusion 3:** The mobile-first design approach was the right choice for the Cameroon market. Most users access the internet through their phones, so the system needed to work well on small screens. The responsive design I implemented achieved this goal.

**Conclusion 4:** The testing results showed that the system performs well with a high success rate. Users who tested the system found it easy to use and valuable. This confirms that the system meets user needs.

**Conclusion 5:** The project demonstrates that it is possible to adapt global e-commerce practices to local African contexts. By considering factors like mobile usage, WhatsApp integration, and local payment preferences, I created a system that fits the specific needs of Cameroonians.

Overall, I successfully achieved the main objective of this study: to design and implement a web-based car parts availability and price comparison system that helps spare parts shops in Yaoundé serve their customers better.

## 5.4 Discussion

During this project, I gained valuable insights and lessons that I want to share:

**Lesson 1: Understanding user needs is crucial.** I spent time observing and interviewing people at John Automobile. This helped me understand their real problems. Without this understanding, I would have built a system that does not solve actual problems.

**Lesson 2: Simple design works best.** I designed the system to be simple and easy to use. Complex features can confuse users. By focusing on core functionalities like search and price comparison, I created a system that users can understand quickly.

**Lesson 3: Mobile-first approach is necessary in Africa.** Most Cameroonians use their phones for internet access. Designing for mobile first ensured that the system works on the devices that most people use.

**Lesson 4: Testing reveals hidden problems.** Through testing, I found and fixed several issues that I did not anticipate. This shows how important it is to test thoroughly before deploying a system.

**Lesson 5: Project management skills are essential.** Managing time, tasks, and resources was challenging but valuable. I learned to break big tasks into smaller ones and work systematically.

These lessons will help me in future projects and in my career as a software engineer.

## 5.5 Recommendations

Based on my experience and the limitations I identified, I recommend the following improvements for future development:

### 5.5.1 Recommendations for System Improvement

**1. Add online payment integration**
Currently, buyers contact vendors through WhatsApp to arrange payment. Adding mobile money integration like Orange Money or MTN Mobile Money would make transactions smoother and more secure.

**2. Add delivery service**
Many buyers prefer to have parts delivered to their location. Partnering with delivery companies or creating an internal delivery system would add great value to the platform.

**3. Improve the search algorithm**
The current search works well, but adding features like spelling correction and smart suggestions would make it even better.

**4. Add push notifications**
Sending notifications when new products match user preferences would keep users engaged and coming back to the platform.

**5. Create a mobile application**
While the website works on mobile, a dedicated mobile app would provide a better user experience and work offline in some functions.

### 5.5.2 Recommendations for Business Development

**1. Expand to other cities**
After testing in Yaoundé, the system could expand to other Cameroonian cities like Douala, Bafoussam, and Limbe.

**2. Partner with spare parts associations**
Working with established associations would help gain trust and attract more vendors to the platform.

**3. Offer premium features**
Vendors could pay for premium features like featured listings, analytics reports, and verified badges.

**4. Launch marketing campaigns**
Promoting SparePlug through social media, radio, and local advertisements would help more people learn about the platform.

### 5.5.3 Recommendations for Academic Research

**1. Conduct larger user studies**
Future researchers could conduct studies with more users to get better data about system effectiveness.

**2. Compare with similar systems**
Research comparing SparePlug with similar platforms in other African countries could provide insights into best practices.

**3. Study adoption factors**
More research on what factors influence users to adopt digital platforms in Cameroon would help improve future systems.

**4. Explore AI integration**
Future projects could explore using artificial intelligence for product recommendations and price prediction.
