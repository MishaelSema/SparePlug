## 2.1 Conceptual Framework

### 2.1.1 E-commerce and Online Marketplaces

E-commerce refers to the buying and selling of goods and services over the internet. Online marketplaces, a subset of e-commerce, are platforms that facilitate transactions between multiple buyers and sellers (Chaffey & Ellis-Chadwick, 2019). Unlike traditional e-commerce sites where a single business sells directly to customers, marketplaces aggregate multiple sellers, providing a centralized platform for discovery and transactions.

Key characteristics of online marketplaces include:
- **Multi-sided platform**: Serves both buyers and sellers simultaneously
- **Value creation**: Creates value by reducing transaction costs and information asymmetry
- **Network effects**: Becomes more valuable as more users join
- **Trust mechanisms**: Implements verification, reviews, and ratings to build trust

### 2.1.2 Marketplace Business Models

Marketplaces typically operate using various revenue models:

1. **Commission-Based**: Take a percentage of each transaction
2. **Subscription-Based**: Charge vendors monthly/annual fees for platform access
3. **Listing Fees**: Charge for each product listing
4. **Featured Listings**: Charge for premium placement
5. **Freemium Model**: Offer basic features free with paid premium tiers

SparePlug adopts a **freemium subscription model** combined with featured listing fees, aligning with the Cameroonian market's preference for direct transactions off-platform.

### 2.1.3 Trust and Verification in Marketplaces

Trust is fundamental to marketplace success. Eisenhardt (1989) identifies information asymmetry as a primary barrier to trust in digital transactions. Marketplaces address this through:

- **Identity Verification**: Confirming seller identity and business legitimacy
- **Reputation Systems**: Reviews and ratings from previous transactions
- **Transparency**: Clear product information, pricing, and seller details
- **Dispute Resolution**: Mechanisms for handling conflicts

SparePlug implements vendor verification through document review (business registration, tax ID) and admin approval, building trust without processing payments.

### 2.1.4 Niche Marketplaces

Niche marketplaces focus on specific product categories or market segments, offering advantages over general platforms:
- **Targeted audience**: Attracts users with specific needs
- **Expertise**: Platform understands industry-specific requirements
- **Better matching**: Specialized search and filtering
- **Community building**: Creates focused user communities

The auto spare parts market represents a niche with specific needs: vehicle compatibility, part condition, seller credibility, and direct communication preferences.

## 2.2 Theoretical Framework

### 2.2.1 Transaction Cost Theory

Coase (1937) introduced transaction cost theory, explaining that firms exist to minimize transaction costs. In the context of marketplaces:

**Transaction Costs Reduced:**
- **Search Costs**: Buyers find vendors more efficiently
- **Information Costs**: Centralized product and pricing information
- **Negotiation Costs**: Clear pricing reduces haggling
- **Enforcement Costs**: Platform provides verification and reviews

**Car Parts Availability and Price Comparison System Application:**
- Reduces search time from days to minutes
- Provides transparent pricing
- Offers vendor credibility information
- Maintains low transaction costs by facilitating, not processing, transactions

### 2.2.2 Network Effects Theory

Metcalfe's Law (Gilder, 1993) states that the value of a network increases proportionally to the square of the number of users. For marketplaces:

**Direct Network Effects**: More buyers attract more sellers, and vice versa
**Indirect Network Effects**: More products attract more buyers, more reviews increase trust

**System Strategy:**
- Free tier attracts initial vendors
- Vendor verification builds trust, attracting buyers
- More buyers create value for vendors, attracting more sellers
- Reviews and ratings create positive feedback loops

### 2.2.3 Two-Sided Market Theory

Rochet and Tirole (2003) developed two-sided market theory, explaining platforms that serve two distinct user groups with interdependent demands.

**Two-Sided Market Characteristics:**
- Two distinct user groups (buyers and sellers)
- Cross-side network effects (value to one group depends on other group's participation)
- Platform pricing must balance both sides
- Platform must attract both sides simultaneously

**System Implementation:**
- **Buyer Side**: Free access to search, browse, contact vendors
- **Vendor Side**: Tiered subscriptions (Free/Basic/Premium) with different features
- **Value Proposition**: More buyers for vendors, more products for buyers

### 2.2.4 Information Systems Success Model

DeLone and McLean (2003) proposed the Information Systems Success Model, identifying six dimensions of IS success:

1. **System Quality**: Technical performance, reliability, response time
2. **Information Quality**: Accuracy, completeness, relevance
3. **Service Quality**: Support, training, service delivery
4. **Use**: System usage patterns
5. **User Satisfaction**: User attitudes toward the system
6. **Net Benefits**: Individual and organizational impacts

**System Design Considerations:**
- Fast, responsive interface (system quality)
- Accurate product information and compatibility (information quality)
- Clear documentation and support (service quality)
- Intuitive, easy-to-use interface (use)
- Positive user experience (satisfaction)
- Business growth for vendors, convenience for buyers (net benefits)

### 2.2.5 Technology Acceptance Model (TAM)

Davis (1989) developed TAM, suggesting that system usage is determined by:
- **Perceived Usefulness**: Belief that using the system will enhance performance
- **Perceived Ease of Use**: Belief that using the system will be free of effort

**Car Parts Availability and Price Comparison System Application:**
- Mobile-first design (ease of use)
- Quick search results (usefulness)
- Simple vendor registration (ease of use)
- Direct communication tools (usefulness)

## 2.3 Related Works

### 2.3.1 Global Marketplace Platforms

**Amazon Marketplace** (Amazon, 2024): The world's largest online marketplace, demonstrating successful multi-vendor platform architecture. Lessons applicable to SparePlug:
- Comprehensive search and filtering
- Vendor verification and ratings
- Tiered vendor programs
- Mobile-first design

**eBay** (eBay Inc., 2024): Pioneered peer-to-peer marketplace model. Relevant features:
- Auction and fixed-price listings
- Buyer and seller ratings
- Category-specific organization
- Payment processing integration

**OLX and Craigslist** (Prosus N.V., 2024): Classified advertising platforms connecting buyers and sellers. Similarities to SparePlug:
- Connector model (facilitates, doesn't process)
- Direct communication between parties
- Local/geographic focus
- No payment processing on platform

### 2.3.2 African and Cameroonian E-commerce

**Jumia** (Jumia Group, 2024): Pan-African e-commerce platform operating in multiple countries. Insights:
- Local market adaptation necessary
- Payment method diversity (mobile money)
- Logistics challenges in Africa
- Mobile-first approach essential

**Konga** (Konga Online Shopping, 2024): Nigerian e-commerce platform. Similarities:
- Marketplace model with multiple vendors
- Local business focus
- Mobile money integration

**Cameroonian Context**: Limited research exists on specialized marketplaces in Cameroon. SparePlug addresses this gap by focusing specifically on auto spare parts, adapting to local preferences for direct communication and off-platform transactions.

### 2.3.3 Auto Parts E-commerce Platforms

- Store locator integration
- Part number search

**PartsGeek** (PartsGeek, 2024): Online auto parts marketplace. Relevant features:
- Vehicle-specific part matching
- Multiple vendor listings

**SparePlug Differentiation**: Focuses on Cameroonian market, connects local vendors with buyers, supports direct communication, and adapts to local payment and communication preferences.
